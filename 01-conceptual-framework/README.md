# Conceptual Framework of Global Leadership Competencies

## 1. Introduction

Global Leadership Competencies (GLCs) provide a conceptual foundation for understanding the capabilities international university students need to engage effectively and responsibly in cross-cultural and global environments.

This framework was developed and examined as part of doctoral research on student affairs management strategies for international undergraduate programs in Beijing, China.

## 2. Six Competency Dimensions

The research framework comprises six dimensions:

### GM — Global Mindset
A global orientation that supports openness to diverse perspectives and understanding of interconnected global contexts.

### ST — Self-Transcendence
An orientation toward personal growth, broader collective interests, and values extending beyond immediate self-interest.

### GSR — Global Social Responsibility
An awareness of ethical responsibilities and the social consequences of decisions and actions in global contexts.

### CCC — Cross-Cultural Competence
The ability to understand cultural differences and engage constructively with people from diverse cultural backgrounds.

### GIC — Global Interpersonal Competence
Interpersonal capabilities that support communication, collaboration, and relationship-building across cultural and national boundaries.

### GSI — Global Strategic Intelligence
The capacity to understand complex global environments and apply strategic thinking to challenges and opportunities.

## 3. Application to Student Affairs Management

The framework provides a basis for examining how university student affairs practices can support competency development across the international student experience.

Relevant management domains include student transition, developmental support, co-curricular engagement, and future preparation.

## 4. Research Development

The six-dimensional framework informed the study's research instrument, empirical analysis, needs assessment, and subsequent strategy development.

Further documentation will describe the theoretical sources, operational definitions, and empirical validation of each dimension.
